The date **30, 60 or 90 days from today** is found by counting forward that many calendar days, with today as day 0. Use the links below for the exact dates right now — they update every day.

## Worked example

Starting on **Sunday, November 15, 2026**:

| Count | Date | Weekday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 days | December 15, 2026 | Tuesday |
| 60 days | January 14, 2027 | Thursday |
| 90 days | February 13, 2027 | Saturday |

Notice that 90 days lands on a Saturday. If you need a working day, the next one is Monday, February 15, 2027 — which is also Presidents' Day in the US, so a US office may not reopen until Tuesday.

## Where these numbers come up

- **Invoices:** "Net 30" and "Net 60" mean payment is due 30 or 60 days after the invoice date.
- **Probation and review periods:** many employers use 30-, 60- or 90-day check-ins for new hires.
- **Return windows:** many stores allow returns within 30 or 90 days.
- **Planning:** "30-60-90 day plans" split a new job or project into three phases.

## Is 90 days the same as 3 months?

Not exactly. Three calendar months can be 89 to 92 days long depending on which months they cover. If a document says "3 months", count months; if it says "90 days", count days.

## FAQ

### Does day 1 start today or tomorrow?

Normally tomorrow. Today is day 0.

### What if the deadline falls on a weekend?

That depends on the rule you're following. Many legal and business rules move the deadline to the next business day — check the wording.
